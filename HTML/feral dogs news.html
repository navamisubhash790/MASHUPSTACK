Guidelines For Managing Feral Dogs

The Supreme Court on Monday agreed to hear a plea seeking directions to the Centre to frame guidelines within a fixed timeline for the management of feral, free-ranging and domestic dogs in wildlife and protected areas. A bench of Justices Vikram Nath and Aparesh Kumar Singh issued notices to the Centre, the National Board for Wildlife and the Animal Welfare Board of India.The petition, filed by the Bombay Natural History Society and others, said free-ranging and feral dogs pose a grave threat to native wildlife in India, including Schedule I species such as the snow leopard, chinkara, leopard cat, great Indian bustard and olive ridley turtle. Senior advocate Arvind Datar, appearing for the petitioners, told the court there have been attacks on endangered species such as snow leopards in Ladakh and Arunachal Pradesh and great Indian bustards in Rajasthan. The matter has been posted for hearing on November 17. The plea, filed through advocate Madhusmita Bora, has sought a direction to the respondents to frame and submit appropriate guidelines before the court within a stipulated timeline. It has also sought the identification of feral, free-ranging and domestic dogs entering wildlife habitats and protected forests through regular monitoring and geo-tagging.
